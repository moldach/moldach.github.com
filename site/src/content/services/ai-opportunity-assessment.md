You do not need to start with a tool. Start with a piece of work that takes too long, creates avoidable errors, or depends on someone remembering the next step. I help small businesses assess that workflow and decide whether AI, ordinary automation, or a clearer process is the useful next move.

## Who this is for

An owner or small team with a recurring problem and enough access to the people doing the work to understand it. You might be rebuilding reports, finding information across documents, preparing repeated paperwork or handing tasks between disconnected tools. These are examples of problems to assess, not claims of completed client deployments.

## What you leave with

- A map of the current workflow, including inputs, decisions, handoffs and exceptions.
- A short, prioritised opportunity list with the reasoning behind each recommendation.
- A feasibility assessment covering source data, integrations, privacy constraints and human review.
- A bounded brief for the first useful improvement, including what success would mean and how to check it.

The outcome can be a recommendation to simplify the process before buying or building anything. An assessment does not commit you to implementation.

## How we work remotely

We begin with a working conversation and a walkthrough of one real task. You explain what arrives, what happens next and where work gets stuck. We use screen sharing and agreed examples, then review the findings together. Sensitive material stays out of public case studies and is handled only through an agreed working arrangement.

You retain the assessment and can use it to prioritise internal work, commission a build, or decide to leave a workflow alone.

Scope depends on the number of workflows, the people involved, the systems to inspect and whether usable examples already exist. After the initial discussion, we agree the deliverables and a written scope before paid work begins. There is no published fixed price or promised return on investment.

## Questions before starting

### Do we need clean data first?

Not necessarily. The assessment identifies whether data quality is the limiting factor and what would need to change before a reliable implementation is possible.

### Will you recommend AI for everything?

No. A deterministic rule, integration or smaller process change may be easier to operate and verify. The recommendation explains that choice.
